Figure 1. gBlock design and synthesis pipeline. Five sequential steps convert reference genomic sequences into synthetic DNA positive controls suitable for diagnostic PCR validation. Each gBlock includes the target amplicon flanked by ~140 bp of genomic sequence for PCR stability and specificity. Quality metrics ensure synthesis feasibility: GC content 25-75%, homopolymer runs <10 bp, and total length 500-3000 bp (optimal for IDT synthesis).

Figure 2: Mutation Context Alignments for All Five Disorders

A. Brachyspina (FANCI) — Exon 25-27 Deletion (3,345 bp)

Wild-Type (NC_037348.1): 5'- ...EXON24[CAGTAGTTGCAGCTCCGGGCTCTAGAGCAC]...EXONS_25-27...[AGCTCAAGTAGTTAGTTGCTCCACTGTACTG]EXON28+ -3' └─ Exon 24 tail (kept) Δ 3,345 bp Exon 28 head (kept) AcrossDEL gBlock: 5'- [EXON24_TAIL]→[EXON28_HEAD] = 409 bp product └─ Direct junction simulates homozygous deletion

B. CVM (SLC35A3) — Point Mutation (G→T at codon 187)

Wild-Type context (AY160683.1, pos 9870): 5'- ATG GCA [G] TTC TAC AGC ATG T -3' ↑ Position 559 CVM mutant context: 5'- ATG GCA [T] TTC TAC AGC ATG T -3' ↑ G→T (codon 187) PIRA Detection (Kanae 2005): Forward primer: CACAATTTGTAGGTCTCACTGCA ↑ Mismatches CT vs AT WT PCR product: ...CTGCA[G] = CTGCAG (PstI cuts) CVM PCR product: ...CTGCA[T] = CTGCAT (no PstI site)

C. HH1 (APAF1) — Stop-Gain Codon (C→T, p.Gln579X)

Wild-Type (NM_001192283.1, exon 14): 5'- ...GAA GGT GTC CTC TAT GCT G[C]GT... -3' Gln (CAG codon) HH1 mutant: 5'- ...GAA GGT GTC CTC TAT GCT G[T]GT... -3' STOP (TAG codon) Effect: Premature termination codon at position 579 Truncated protein (579 aa → STOP)

D. HH3 (SMC2) — Missense Mutation (T→C, p.Phe1135Ser)

Wild-Type (AC_000165.1, BTA17): 5'- ...TTT GCT [T]TC AAC... -3' Phe (TTC codon) HH3 mutant: 5'- ...TTT GCT [C]TC AAC... -3' Ser (CTC codon) Change: Phenylalanine (hydrophobic) → Serine (hydrophilic)

E. HH4 (GART) — Missense Mutation (A→C, p.Asn290Thr)

Wild-Type (NM_001081610.1): 5'- ...AAC GGT GTC CT[A]TA TGC... -3' Asn (AAC codon) HH4 mutant: 5'- ...AAC GGT GTC CT[C]TA TGC... -3' Thr (ACC codon) MseI sensitivity: A→C may create/destroy MseI site (TTAA)

Figure 2. Mutation context for each bovine congenital disorder. (A) Brachyspina shows a large exonic deletion (3,345 bp spanning exons 25-27), detected by a 409 bp AcrossDEL PCR product that only amplifies from the deletion allele. (B) CVM shows a single nucleotide substitution (G→T) that is detected via primer-introduced restriction analysis (PIRA) using PstI. (C-E) HH1, HH3, and HH4 are point mutations in different genes, detected using PCR-RFLP assays. All mutations are within PCR amplicon regions flanked by 100-150 bp of genomic sequence for synthesis stability.

Figure 4: Assay Strategies and gBlock Applications

Brachyspina FANCI Large deletion (3,345 bp) AcrossDEL PCR Positive control (409 bp only) Confirms deletion-specific amplicon

Figure 4. Summary of assay strategies and gBlock applications. Each disorder requires a different detection method based on mutation type: large deletions use size-specific PCR (AcrossDEL), point mutations typically use PCR-RFLP or primer-introduced restriction analysis (PIRA). gBlocks serve as positive controls for each assay, enabling technicians to validate PCR amplification, restriction enzyme function, and gel interpretation without requiring animal tissue or archived samples.

Figure 6: pJET1.2/Blunt Cloning Strategy for gBlock Validation

Figure 6. pJET1.2/blunt positive selection cloning strategy for gBlock validation. The pJET vector contains an eco47IR lethal gene that is disrupted upon insert ligation, eliminating background colonies without requiring IPTG/X-Gal screening. (Bottom left) Empty vector result in cell death. (Bottom right) Inserted gBlock inactivates the lethal gene, allowing colony growth. All colonies recovered after transformation contain the insert, enabling rapid validation via colony PCR using pJET sequencing primers.
